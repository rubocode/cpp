## Introduction
> We must free ourselves of the hope that the sea will ever rest.
> We must learn to sail in high winds.  
> **Aristotle Onassis**

The C Programming Language was created together with the UNIX Operating System by Ken Thompson and Dennis Ritchie at AT&T Bell Laboratories in Murray Hill, New Jersey during the late 1960s and early 1970s.

Bjarne Stroustrup joined the very same Bell Laboratories in 1979 and had been influenced by the Simula Programming Language.  He found the concept of classes in Simula intriguing and also realized that Simula was not efficient enough for the research work he planned to do at Bell Labs.  Since C provided an efficient machine abstraction, he thought he should built a new language based on C, but now with classes added.  This language was initially called C with Classes and later renamed as C++.

The language evolved as a general and comprehensive toolkit that enabled user-defined first-clasas abstractions.  It supported procedural, object-oriented and generic programming paradigms while keeping performance as top priority.  Then a standardization process and a standards committee were formed to enable wide adoption internationally.

Initial standardization was done in 1998, followed by updates in 2003 and 2011.  After that point, the committee decided to update the standard evey 3 years leading to updates in 2014, 2017, 2020 and 2023. Unfortunately, the language has become quite complex due to the need to support many different ways of doing the same thing to accommodate backward compatiility for existing large code bases.  We should not lose sight of the fact that, it is now an effort of the variety known as design by committee.  Beginning as a research language and having significant impact and becoming ubiquitous, it is now a general purpose programming language for specialists.

As the language has evolved to be rather complex Stroustrup's books became huge blocks of paper until he got tired of writing them (C++11 was the last one).  Then he started a series called A Tour of C++ with each new standard every three years.  You had to have lived with the language through all the standards to use these books optimally.   You no longer have the full view in one place.

However, it is useful to understand the essence of the language in a way that an individual can grasp it and confidently program in it.  For somebody who is familiar with C++ but doesn't have the full scope of the evolution.  This guide is my attempt to capture the essence of the language in order that all the root elements are understood.  If I could capture a stable perspective concisely, it would be worth the effort.  What is captured and what is left out would be art, not technology.  Enjoy!
